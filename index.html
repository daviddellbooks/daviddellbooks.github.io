About the book

From the book description

What if everything you thought you knew about UFOs was wrong? For decades, we've stared at the skies, dreaming of alien visitors crossing the stars. Are UFOs fallen angels?

Background note (our commentary, not a summary of the book)

It's an old question in new clothes. Genesis 6:4 says "There were giants in the earth in those days; and also after that, when the sons of God came in unto the daughters of men" (KJV). Many early Jewish and Christian readers took those "sons of God" to be angels, the Watchers described at length in the Book of Enoch. Later, some secular UFO researchers concluded the phenomenon behaves less like spacecraft and more like the visitations found in folklore.

That background is ours. The book starts from its own question, “What if everything you thought you knew about UFOs was wrong?”, and dares you to “rethink the lights—and the shadows they cast.” Skeptics and believers are both welcome to weigh the case.

"Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world." 1 John 4:1 (KJV)

After the UFO hearings: the question nobody asked

Our commentary

On July 26, 2023, a House Oversight subcommittee heard sworn testimony about UFOs (now officially "UAP"). A retired Navy commander, David Fravor, described a white "Tic Tac" object off San Diego in 2004. Former Navy pilot Ryan Graves described "a dark gray or a black cube inside of a clear sphere." Former intelligence officer David Grusch alleged "a multi-decade UAP crash retrieval and reverse engineering program," while saying of the alleged bodies, "That is something I have not witnessed myself."

Skeptics spoke up in the same hearing. Rep. Eric Burlison put it plainly: “I am pretty skeptical... You have got to show me, right.” And the U.S. Air Force says the 1947 Roswell debris came from Project Mogul, a classified balloon program.

So nothing has been proven, yet people keep seeing something. The hearing notes above are our own summary of the public record. The book's view, in the words of its description, is that “the lights we chase aren't ships from distant planets—they're glimpses of an interdimensional realm, home to the fallen angels of biblical lore.”
